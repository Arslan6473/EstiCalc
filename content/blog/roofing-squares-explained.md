Roofers don't talk in square feet; they talk in **squares**. A quote that says "22 squares, architectural shingles" is giving you the size of the job in a unit that maps directly to how shingles are packaged and how labor is priced. Once you understand squares and how roof pitch affects them, you can check a bid, order materials and avoid the common mistake of measuring only the house footprint.

This guide covers what a square is, how to measure your roof's surface area safely, pitch multipliers, bundles, and the accessories that are easy to forget. The [Roofing Squares Calculator](/calculators/roofing-squares-calculator) and [Shingles Calculator](/calculators/shingles-calculator) handle the arithmetic.

## What is a roofing square?

A roofing square is **100 square feet of roof surface**. A roof with 2,400 square feet of surface is 24 squares. The unit exists because shingles have long been packaged so that a set number of bundles covers one square.

| Roof surface (sq ft) | Squares |
|---|---|
| 1,000 | 10 |
| 1,500 | 15 |
| 2,000 | 20 |
| 2,750 | 27.5 |

## Footprint vs actual roof surface

The area of your house on a plan is not the area of your roof. Two things make the roof larger:

1. **Overhangs.** Eaves and rakes extend past the walls, often by 12 inches or more.
2. **Slope.** A sloped plane is longer than the horizontal distance it covers.

You can account for both by measuring the footprint *including overhangs* and then multiplying by a **pitch multiplier**.

## Understanding roof pitch

Pitch is the vertical rise per 12 inches of horizontal run, written as rise/12. A 6/12 roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of run.

![Pitch triangle showing 12 inches of run, 6 inches of rise and a sloped length of 13.42 inches, giving a 6/12 pitch multiplier of 1.118](/images/blog/roofing-squares-explained/diagram-1.svg)

The multiplier is the sloped length divided by the run:

**Multiplier = √(12² + rise²) ÷ 12**

For a 6/12 roof: √(144 + 36) ÷ 12 = √180 ÷ 12 = 13.416 ÷ 12 = **1.118**.

| Pitch | Multiplier | Approx. angle |
|---|---|---|
| 3/12 | 1.031 | 14.0° |
| 4/12 | 1.054 | 18.4° |
| 5/12 | 1.083 | 22.6° |
| 6/12 | 1.118 | 26.6° |
| 7/12 | 1.158 | 30.3° |
| 8/12 | 1.202 | 33.7° |
| 9/12 | 1.250 | 36.9° |
| 10/12 | 1.302 | 39.8° |
| 12/12 | 1.414 | 45.0° |

### Measuring pitch from the ground or attic

You don't need to climb onto the roof to find the pitch:

- **From the attic:** Hold a level horizontally against the underside of a rafter, mark 12 inches along the level, and measure straight up from that mark to the rafter. That vertical distance is the rise.
- **From a gable end:** Many smartphone level apps and digital angle finders can measure the angle of the rake trim from a ladder at the gable, which you can convert to pitch.
- **From plans:** House plans often list the pitch on the elevations.

## Step-by-step: estimate your roof in squares

1. **Measure the footprint including overhangs.** Measure the house walls from the ground and add the overhang on each side.
2. **Calculate the footprint area** in square feet. Split L-shapes into rectangles; see [how to calculate square footage](/blog/how-to-calculate-square-footage).
3. **Multiply by the pitch multiplier** to get roof surface area.
4. **Divide by 100** to get squares.
5. **Add waste:** about 10% for a simple gable, about 15% for hips, valleys and dormers.
6. **Round up** to the next full or third of a square, depending on how your supplier sells.

### Worked example: a simple gable roof

A house measures 40 × 30 feet with 1-foot overhangs all around, and a 6/12 roof.

![Gable roof plan 42 by 32 feet including overhangs, with ridge along the 42 foot length and calculation of 15.0 squares before waste](/images/blog/roofing-squares-explained/diagram-2.svg)

- Footprint with overhangs: (40 + 2) × (30 + 2) = 42 × 32 = **1,344 sq ft**
- Roof surface: 1,344 × 1.118 = **1,502.6 sq ft**
- Squares: 1,502.6 ÷ 100 = **15.03 squares**
- With 10% waste: 15.03 × 1.10 = **16.5 squares**
- Shingle bundles at 3 per square: 16.5 × 3 = 49.6 → **50 bundles**

Using the rule for a gable: each rafter run is half the 32-foot width, or 16 feet, and the sloped length is 16 × 1.118 = **17.9 feet** from eave edge to ridge.

## Bundles per square

Most standard asphalt shingles, both three-tab and architectural (laminated), come **three bundles per square**. Heavier designer shingles may need four or five bundles per square. The coverage is printed on every wrapper. Don't assume; check the exact product.

## Accessories you'll need

Shingles are only part of the order. Using the same 42 × 32 gable:

| Item | How to measure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Starter strip | Length of eaves (plus rakes if your shingle maker recommends it) | 2 × 42 = 84 ft of eaves |
| Ridge cap | Length of ridges and hips | 42 ft of ridge |
| Drip edge | Eaves plus rakes, in 10-ft pieces with overlap | 84 + (4 × 17.9) = 155.6 ft → about 16–17 pieces |
| Underlayment | Roof surface area ÷ roll coverage | 1,503 sq ft ÷ roll coverage on label |
| Ice and water barrier | Eaves (in cold climates, per code) and valleys | Typically along the 84 ft of eaves |
| Nails | Per shingle maker's nailing pattern | Usually 4–6 nails per shingle |
| Vents, flashing, boots | Count each penetration | Pipe boots, step flashing at walls |

Ridge cap and starter coverage per bundle varies a lot by product, so use the label rather than a rule of thumb.

## Complicated roofs

Hip roofs, valleys, dormers and multiple levels make measurement harder:

- **Break the roof into planes** (rectangles, triangles and trapezoids), compute each plane's horizontal area, and apply the multiplier for its pitch.
- **Hip roofs** at the same pitch on every side have the same total surface area as a gable roof over the same footprint and pitch, but they produce more cut waste and need more ridge cap along the hips.
- **Different pitches** on different sections need separate multipliers.
- **Satellite or drone measurement reports** are widely used by roofers; ask your contractor if they used one.

## Checking a roofing bid

With your own estimate in hand, you can sanity-check quotes:

- Is the quoted number of squares close to yours (after waste)?
- Does it include tear-off of existing layers and disposal?
- Are underlayment, ice barrier, drip edge, flashing and ventilation listed?
- Is decking replacement priced per sheet in case rot is found?

## Roofing safety note

Roof work is one of the most dangerous home-improvement tasks because of fall risk. Measure from the ground, attic or plans wherever possible. If you must go up, use a properly secured ladder extending at least 3 feet above the roof edge, wear soft-soled shoes, and use a fall-protection harness and anchor on any pitched roof. Steeper roofs (generally above about 6/12) are hard to walk safely without roof jacks or staging. Never work on a wet, icy or windy roof, stay well clear of overhead power lines, and consider hiring a licensed, insured roofer for anything beyond measuring.

## Quick checklist

- One square = 100 sq ft of roof surface.
- Measure footprint including overhangs.
- Multiply by the pitch multiplier, then divide by 100.
- Add 10% waste for gables, 15% for complex roofs.
- Usually 3 bundles per square; confirm on the wrapper.
- Estimate starter, ridge cap, drip edge and underlayment separately.
- Measure from the ground whenever you can.

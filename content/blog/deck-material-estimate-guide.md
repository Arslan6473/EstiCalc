A deck is a pile of lumber and a bucket of hardware arranged in a very specific way. Estimating it well means counting each layer separately: the framing that holds it up, the decking you walk on, and the hardware that ties everything together. Miss one category and you're back at the lumber yard mid-build; overbuy and you're stuck with expensive leftovers.

This guide breaks a deck into its parts, shows how to count each one, and works through a complete 12 × 16 foot example. The [Deck Boards Calculator](/calculators/deck-boards-calculator) and [Deck Joists Calculator](/calculators/deck-joists-calculator) handle the counting once you have your dimensions.

## The parts of a deck

From the ground up:

1. **Footings:** concrete footings below the frost line support the posts.
2. **Posts:** typically 4×4 or 6×6, carrying the beam.
3. **Beam:** usually two or three joist-size boards fastened together, spanning between posts.
4. **Ledger:** a board bolted to the house rim joist (for an attached deck) that carries one end of the joists.
5. **Joists:** run between ledger and beam (or beam and beam) at regular spacing.
6. **Rim or band joists:** frame the outer edges.
7. **Decking:** the walking surface.
8. **Hardware:** hangers, post bases, beam connectors, lateral load devices, flashing, screws, bolts and nails.
9. **Railings and stairs:** estimated separately.

## How sizes and spans are determined

Joist size, beam size, post spacing and footing size all depend on the lumber species and grade, the spacing, the design loads and local requirements such as snow load and frost depth. In the US, many jurisdictions base deck rules on the International Residential Code (IRC), and the American Wood Council publishes a widely used guide, *Prescriptive Residential Wood Deck Construction Guide* (DCA 6), with span tables. Use your local code's tables or a design professional to choose sizes, and use this guide for quantities once the design is settled.

## Step 1: Joists

Count joists along the dimension they are spaced across:

**Number of joists = (length ÷ spacing) + 1**

Convert length to inches first. Then add rim joists on the ends if they're not already counted, and any doubled joists (for example, under board butt joints or around stairs).

Common spacing is 16 inches on center. Some decking requires 12 inches, especially composite boards laid on a diagonal; check the decking manufacturer's instructions before you order framing.

## Step 2: Beam, posts and footings

- **Beam length** equals the deck width it supports, plus any overhang allowed by your design.
- **Posts** are spaced according to the beam span table for your beam size.
- **Footings** match the post count. The [Post Hole Calculator](/calculators/post-hole-calculator) estimates concrete per hole, and [how many bags of concrete](/blog/how-many-bags-of-concrete) explains the conversion to bags.

## Step 3: Decking

Decking is estimated by rows. Each row covers the board's actual width plus the gap.

**Rows = deck width (in) ÷ (board width + gap)**

Nominal 6-inch decking (2×6 or 5/4×6) is typically 5.5 inches wide. Gaps vary by material and moisture content; around 1/8 to 1/4 inch is common, and composites specify their own gap. With a 3/16-inch gap, each row covers 5.6875 inches.

### Board length strategy

- If a standard length covers the run (8, 10, 12, 16 or 20 feet), one board per row is the simplest and produces the least waste.
- If not, plan butt joints that land over a joist (often a doubled joist or blocking) and stagger them so joints don't line up in adjacent rows.
- A picture-frame border or diagonal pattern increases waste, typically 10–15% for diagonals.

For general guidance on waste allowances, see the [material waste factor guide](/blog/material-waste-factor-guide).

## Step 4: Fasteners

### Deck screws

Face-screwed decking typically uses two screws at every joist crossing:

**Screws = rows × joists crossed × 2**

That works out to roughly 350 screws per 100 square feet with 5.5-inch boards on 16-inch spacing. Buy about 5% extra for dropped and stripped screws.

### Hidden fasteners

Clip systems are counted per joist crossing (often one clip per crossing) and come in boxes rated for a certain square footage. Follow the maker's chart.

### Structural hardware

- **Joist hangers:** one per joist end that attaches to a ledger or flush beam.
- **Hanger fasteners:** use the nails or structural screws specified by the hanger maker, in the quantity printed on the hanger or catalog.
- **Ledger fasteners:** lag screws or through-bolts in the size and spacing pattern given by your code.
- **Beam-to-post connectors** and **post bases.**
- **Hurricane ties or joist-to-beam connectors** where required.
- **Lateral load connectors** where required by code for attached decks.
- **Ledger flashing** to keep water out of the house framing.

Fasteners and connectors used with pressure-treated lumber need adequate corrosion resistance, typically hot-dip galvanized or stainless steel; check the lumber and connector makers' recommendations.

## Worked example: a 12 × 16 foot attached deck

Design assumptions (yours will differ; confirm sizes with your local code tables):

- Deck is 16 feet along the house and projects 12 feet.
- Joists span 12 feet from a ledger to a beam, 16 inches on center.
- Decking runs parallel to the house using 16-foot boards, 5.5 inches wide with 3/16-inch gaps.
- The design calls for a beam on three posts.

### Framing

| Item | Calculation | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Joists (12 ft) | (16 × 12 in ÷ 16 in) + 1 = 12 + 1 | 13 |
| Ledger (16 ft) | One board along the house | 1 |
| Rim joist (16 ft) | Across the joist ends | 1 |
| Beam (16 ft) | Built-up: 2 plies | 2 |
| Posts | Per design | 3 |
| Footings | One per post | 3 |

The two outer joists in the count of 13 double as the side rims.

### Decking

- Rows: 144 in ÷ 5.6875 in = 25.3 → **26 rows**
- Boards: 26 × 16-ft boards, plus 2 spares = **28 boards**
- Area: 12 × 16 = **192 sq ft**

### Fasteners and hardware

| Item | Calculation | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| Deck screws | 26 rows × 13 joists × 2 = 676, +5% | about 710 |
| Joist hangers | One per joist at the ledger | 13 |
| Joist-to-beam ties | One per joist at the beam (if required) | 13 |
| Post bases and post-to-beam connectors | One each per post | 3 + 3 |
| Ledger fasteners | Per code spacing along 16 ft | per table |
| Ledger flashing | 16 ft, plus laps | 2 pieces of 10 ft |

At roughly 350 screws per 100 sq ft, 192 sq ft suggests about 670, which matches the row-by-row count. Remember stair stringers, treads and railing posts, balusters and rails are additional.

## Structural safety note

A deck is a structure that carries people, often well above the ground. Deck collapses are frequently traced to inadequate ledger attachment, missing flashing that lets the house rim joist rot, undersized footings or improper connectors. Check with your building department before you start: many jurisdictions require a permit and inspections for decks, particularly attached or raised ones. Follow the span tables and fastening schedules in your local code, use connectors exactly as their manufacturer specifies, never attach a ledger to brick veneer or a cantilevered floor without engineered details, and call 811 before digging footings. If the design is outside the prescriptive tables, consult a licensed engineer.

## Quick checklist

- Confirm joist, beam and post sizes from code tables first.
- Joists = (length ÷ spacing) + 1, plus rims and doubles.
- Decking rows = width ÷ (board width + gap).
- Screws = rows × joists × 2, plus 5%.
- Count every hanger, connector and the fasteners each requires.
- Use corrosion-resistant hardware rated for treated lumber.
- Pull permits and follow your local code.

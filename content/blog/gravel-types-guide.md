Walk into a landscape supply yard and ask for "gravel" and you'll get a question back: *which one?* Gravel products differ in size, shape and whether they contain fine particles, and those differences decide whether a driveway stays firm, a path feels comfortable underfoot, or a drain actually drains. Ordering the wrong type is an expensive mistake because it's heavy to move and hard to remove.

This guide explains the most common gravel types, which to use where, and how to calculate cubic yards and tons. When you're ready to order, the [Gravel Calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator) does the volume and weight math.

## Two properties that matter most

### Angular vs rounded

- **Crushed stone** has sharp, fractured faces. Pieces lock together when compacted, so it stays put under load. Driveways, bases and parking pads need angular stone.
- **Rounded stone** (pea gravel, river rock) has been tumbled smooth by water. It's comfortable underfoot and attractive, but the pieces roll past each other, so it shifts and ruts under vehicles.

### Clean vs with fines

- **Clean (washed)** stone has the dust and small particles removed. The spaces between stones stay open, so water drains through. It doesn't compact into a solid mass.
- **Stone with fines** contains everything from dust up to the top size. The fines fill voids and compact into a hard, stable layer, but water drains through it slowly.

## Common gravel types

Names and size numbers vary by region and supplier. The "#" sizes come from standard aggregate gradations, but local yards may use their own names, so always ask to see the product or a size chart.

| Type | Typical size | Shape / fines | Best uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crusher run (dense-grade aggregate, also sold as "ABC," "road base" or "3/4-minus") | Dust up to about 3/4 in | Angular, with fines | Driveway and patio base, top dressing for gravel drives, under pavers |
| #57 stone | About 1/2 to 1 in | Angular, clean | Driveway middle layer, drainage, French drains, under sheds |
| #3 stone | About 1 to 2 in | Angular, clean | Driveway base layer, construction entrances |
| #411 | #57 stone mixed with stone dust | Angular, with fines | Driveway top layer that compacts and still has larger stone |
| Pea gravel | About 3/8 in | Rounded, clean | Paths, play areas, patios with edging, dog runs |
| River rock | About 3/4 in up to several inches | Rounded, clean | Dry creek beds, decorative borders, downspout splash areas |
| Decomposed granite (DG) | Fine particles up to about 1/4 in | Granular, with fines | Paths and patios with a natural compacted surface |
| Stone dust / screenings | Dust to about 1/8 in | Fine | Leveling bed under pavers or flagstone, fill for joints |

## Choosing gravel by project

### Driveways

A gravel driveway is a layered system. Each layer has a job:

![Cross-section of a gravel driveway: compacted subgrade, 4 to 6 inches of #3 stone base, 4 inches of #57 stone, and 2 to 3 inches of crusher run on top, crowned for drainage](/images/blog/gravel-types-guide/diagram-1.svg)

1. **Subgrade:** remove topsoil and organic material, then compact the soil. Geotextile fabric over soft soil helps keep stone from sinking.
2. **Base layer:** larger crushed stone such as #3, often 4–6 inches, for load-bearing.
3. **Middle layer:** #57 stone, roughly 4 inches.
4. **Top layer:** crusher run, #411 or a similar stone with fines, 2–3 inches, compacted to a firm surface.
5. **Crown:** shape the surface so it's higher in the middle and water sheds to the sides.

Depths vary with soil, climate and vehicle weight. Clay soils and heavy trucks call for thicker bases.

### Walkways and garden paths

- **Pea gravel** is comfortable and affordable but needs solid edging, and it can move with foot traffic. A compacted crushed-stone base under 1–2 inches of pea gravel helps.
- **Decomposed granite** compacts into a firm, natural-looking surface. Stabilized DG (with a binder) resists erosion better.
- Edging is essential for both; the [Edging Calculator](/calculators/edging-calculator) gives the linear feet.

### Drainage

For French drains, behind retaining walls, under downspouts and around foundation drains, use **clean, washed angular stone** such as #57. Wrap the trench in non-woven geotextile fabric so soil doesn't clog the voids. Avoid crusher run or anything with fines for drainage work.

### Under pavers and sheds

Pavers usually sit on a compacted base of dense-grade aggregate plus a 1-inch sand or screenings bedding layer; see the [Paver Base Calculator](/calculators/paver-base-calculator). Shed pads often use 4 inches of #57 or crusher run inside a timber frame.

### Decorative ground cover

River rock and decorative stone suit beds and dry creek beds. They're heavy per square foot because they're laid thicker; use the [River Rock Calculator](/calculators/river-rock-calculator) to size an order. If you're comparing stone against organic ground cover, [how much mulch do I need](/blog/how-much-mulch-do-i-need) covers the mulch side.

## How much gravel to order

### Step 1: Calculate volume

Volume (cubic feet) = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft). Convert depth in inches to feet by dividing by 12. Divide cubic feet by 27 to get cubic yards. Our guide on [cubic yards explained](/blog/cubic-yards-explained) goes deeper.

### Step 2: Convert to tons if your supplier sells by weight

Many crushed stone products weigh roughly **1.4 to 1.5 tons per cubic yard**, but this varies. Washed stone, crusher run and river rock all differ, and wet material weighs more. Ask the yard for its conversion.

### Step 3: Add for compaction and loss

Materials with fines compact noticeably. Adding around 10–15% for compacted layers is a common allowance, and a little extra covers uneven subgrade. See the [material waste factor guide](/blog/material-waste-factor-guide).

### Worked example: a 12 × 50 ft driveway top-up

You want to add a 4-inch layer of crusher run to a 12 × 50 foot driveway.

- Area: 12 × 50 = **600 sq ft**
- Depth: 4 in ÷ 12 = **0.333 ft**
- Volume: 600 × 0.333 = **200 cu ft**
- Cubic yards: 200 ÷ 27 = **7.4 cu yd**
- Add 10% for compaction: 7.4 × 1.10 = **8.1 cu yd**
- Approx. weight at 1.45 tons/yd: 8.1 × 1.45 ≈ **11.8 tons**

| Coverage reference | 2 in deep | 3 in deep | 4 in deep |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 cubic yard covers | 162 sq ft | 108 sq ft | 81 sq ft |

## Buying bulk vs bags

Bagged gravel (often 0.5 cubic foot bags) is convenient for small jobs, but 1 cubic yard equals 54 of those bags. For anything more than a cubic yard or two, bulk delivery is usually far cheaper per yard. Check the delivery minimum and fee, how close the truck can get without damaging your lawn or septic system, and whether the driver can spread the load as it dumps.

Plan the drop spot before the truck arrives. A dump truck is heavy and may crack a concrete driveway or sink into soft ground, and a large pile can take hours to move by wheelbarrow.

## Installation tips

- Remove sod and topsoil; organic material under gravel decomposes and creates soft spots.
- Compact each layer separately with a plate compactor, lightly moistening stone with fines.
- Use fabric on soft or clay soils.
- Install edging before spreading the top layer.
- Keep a little extra of the top layer to fill ruts later.

## Safety note

Gravel is heavy: a cubic yard of stone typically weighs well over a ton. Lift with your legs, don't overload wheelbarrows, and use eye protection and a dust mask when spreading dusty material such as crusher run or stone dust, since crushed stone dust can contain crystalline silica. Call 811 before digging a driveway base or drainage trench so buried utilities are marked.

## Quick checklist

- Angular stone for anything that carries weight; rounded stone for comfort and decoration.
- Clean stone for drainage; stone with fines for compacted surfaces.
- Build driveways in layers with a crown.
- Calculate cubic yards, then convert to tons using your supplier's figure.
- Add 10–15% for compaction.
- Plan delivery access and call 811 before digging.

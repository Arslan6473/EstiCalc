Landscape suppliers and concrete plants sell by the cubic yard, but most of us think in square feet and inches. That mismatch is why people either order a truckload twice the size they need or end up making three trips to the garden centre for bags. Once you understand what a cubic yard actually is and how to calculate one, ordering mulch, gravel, topsoil or concrete becomes a two-minute job.

## What is a cubic yard?

A cubic yard is a volume: a cube 1 yard (3 feet) long, 3 feet wide and 3 feet tall. Since 3 × 3 × 3 = 27, **one cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet**.

![A cube 3 feet wide, 3 feet deep and 3 feet tall divided into 27 one-foot cubes, showing that 1 cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet](/images/blog/cubic-yards-explained/cubic-yard-cube.svg)

It helps to picture it: a cubic yard is roughly the size of a large washing machine, or a pile of material that would come up to your thigh if spread over a 3 × 3 ft square. In practice, material dumped from a truck forms a cone, so a yard of mulch on your driveway looks bigger than you expect.

"Yard" on a supplier's price list almost always means cubic yard. If you see "per yard" for mulch, gravel, soil or concrete, it is a volume, not a length.

## The formula

> **Cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (ft) ÷ 27**

The trap is that depth is usually given in inches. Convert it first by dividing by 12:

| Depth | In feet |
|---|---|
| 1 in | 0.083 ft |
| 2 in | 0.167 ft |
| 3 in | 0.25 ft |
| 4 in | 0.333 ft |
| 6 in | 0.5 ft |
| 12 in | 1 ft |

So a 10 × 12 ft area at 3 inches deep is 10 × 12 × 0.25 = 30 cubic feet, and 30 ÷ 27 = **1.11 cubic yards**.

If you prefer to stay in square feet: **cubic yards = square feet × depth (in) ÷ 324**. The 324 comes from 27 × 12. The [cubic yard calculator](/calculators/cubic-yard-calculator) handles all of this, including metric units, and the [cubic feet calculator](/calculators/cubic-feet-calculator) is useful when you are buying bags.

## How far does a cubic yard go?

This table is worth saving. It shows how many square feet one cubic yard covers at common depths:

![Bar chart showing one cubic yard covers 324 square feet at 1 inch, 162 at 2 inches, 108 at 3 inches, 81 at 4 inches and 54 at 6 inches deep](/images/blog/cubic-yards-explained/coverage-by-depth.svg)

| Depth | Area covered by 1 cu yd | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 in | 324 sq ft | Top-dressing lawns with compost |
| 2 in | 162 sq ft | Refreshing existing mulch, decorative stone |
| 3 in | 108 sq ft | New mulch beds, pea gravel paths |
| 4 in | 81 sq ft | Concrete patio slab, gravel driveway top layer |
| 6 in | 54 sq ft | Raised beds, gravel base under pavers or sheds |

Depth matters more than people expect: doubling the depth doubles the volume.

## Shapes other than rectangles

Find the area in square feet first, then multiply by depth:

- **Circle** (tree ring, round bed): 3.1416 × radius² — see our guide on [how to calculate square footage](/blog/how-to-calculate-square-footage) for other shapes.
- **Curvy beds:** Break into rectangles and triangles, or estimate an average width and multiply by length.
- **Sloped excavation:** Use the average depth of the shallow and deep ends.

## Bags vs bulk

Bagged products are sold by cubic feet (mulch, soil, gravel) or by weight (concrete). Here is how they convert:

| Bag size | Bags per cubic yard |
|---|---|
| 0.75 cu ft (common for topsoil and sand) | 36 |
| 1 cu ft | 27 |
| 1.5 cu ft | 18 |
| 2 cu ft (common for mulch) | 13.5 |
| 3 cu ft | 9 |
| 80 lb concrete premix (≈ 0.6 cu ft) | 45 |
| 60 lb concrete premix (≈ 0.45 cu ft) | 60 |

Bags make sense for small jobs, tight access and when you cannot have a pile on the driveway. Bulk delivery usually wins on price once you need more than a yard or two, though delivery fees and minimum orders vary by supplier. For concrete specifically, see [how many bags of concrete](/blog/how-many-bags-of-concrete) for where the break-even typically falls.

## How much does a cubic yard weigh?

Weight matters if you are hauling material yourself. These are rough figures — moisture content changes them significantly, and suppliers often publish their own:

| Material | Approximate weight per cubic yard |
|---|---|
| Wood chip or bark mulch | roughly 400–800 lb |
| Compost | roughly 1,000–1,600 lb |
| Topsoil | roughly 2,000–2,700 lb |
| Gravel or crushed stone | roughly 2,400–3,000 lb |
| Sand | roughly 2,600–3,000 lb |
| Wet concrete | roughly 3,900–4,050 lb |

A single cubic yard of gravel can exceed the payload rating of many light pickup trucks. Check your vehicle's payload (usually on the driver's door-jamb sticker) before loading, and do not rely on "it fits in the bed". If a supplier sells by the ton, ask them for their conversion; our [gravel types guide](/blog/gravel-types-guide) explains why crushed stone and rounded gravel weigh differently.

## Worked examples

### Mulch bed

A bed along the front of the house is 30 ft long and 4 ft deep (front to back), with mulch at 3 inches.

- Area: 30 × 4 = 120 sq ft
- Volume: 120 × 0.25 = 30 cu ft
- Cubic yards: 30 ÷ 27 = **1.11 cu yd**
- In 2 cu ft bags: 30 ÷ 2 = **15 bags**

At this size, bags and a 1-yard bulk delivery are both reasonable. More on this in [how much mulch do I need](/blog/how-much-mulch-do-i-need).

### Gravel driveway top-up

A driveway 60 ft long and 10 ft wide needs 4 inches of gravel.

- Area: 600 sq ft
- Volume: 600 × 0.333 = 200 cu ft
- Cubic yards: 200 ÷ 27 = **7.4 cu yd**
- At about 1.4 tons per yard: roughly **10.4 tons**

Compacted gravel settles, so many suppliers suggest ordering extra for base layers that will be compacted. Ask your supplier how much they recommend for the material you are buying. The [gravel calculator](/calculators/gravel-calculator) converts yards to tons.

### Concrete patio slab

A 10 × 12 ft patio at 4 inches thick:

- Volume: 10 × 12 × 0.333 = 40 cu ft
- Cubic yards: 40 ÷ 27 = **1.48 cu yd**
- With 10% extra for uneven ground and spillage: **1.63 cu yd**

Ready-mix plants usually sell in quarter-yard increments, so you would order 1.75 yards. The [concrete slab calculator](/calculators/concrete-slab-calculator) includes thickness options and waste.

## Ordering tips

- **Round up**, not down. Running short on concrete mid-pour is a real problem; running short on mulch is just annoying.
- **Allow for settling and compaction** with gravel, soil and base materials.
- **Measure depth after removing old material**, or you will order too little.
- **Ask about minimums and delivery fees** — a small bulk order can cost more than bags once delivery is added.
- **Choose where the pile goes.** Put down a tarp to protect the lawn or driveway, and keep it clear of storm drains.

For the difference between soil products, see [topsoil vs garden soil vs compost](/blog/topsoil-vs-garden-soil-vs-compost). Whatever you are ordering, measure length, width and depth, divide by 27, and you will know exactly how much to ask for.

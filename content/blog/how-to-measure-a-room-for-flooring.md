Ordering flooring is unforgiving. Run short by half a box and you may find the same product is out of stock or comes from a different production run with a slightly different colour. Over-order by four boxes and you have spent hundreds on material that sits in the garage. Getting it right comes down to measuring carefully, including the small areas people forget, and then converting square feet into the units you actually buy.

This guide covers the full process, from sketching the room to counting boxes and trim. The [flooring calculator](/calculators/flooring-calculator) will do the conversions for you once you have your measurements.

## What you need

- A 25-ft tape measure (or a laser measure for long rooms)
- Graph paper or a notepad, and a pencil
- A helper for long runs, if possible
- Your chosen product's box coverage (square feet per box), which is printed on the box and the product listing

## Step 1: Sketch the floor plan

Draw a rough plan of every room getting the new floor, including closets, hallways and doorways between rooms. It does not need to be to scale, but every wall should appear on it. Mark where the floor changes to a different material, such as tile in a bathroom.

A sketch prevents the two most common measuring errors: forgetting a closet and counting a hallway twice.

## Step 2: Measure each room's length and width

Measure wall to wall at floor level, ignoring baseboards (the flooring runs under where the baseboard sits, or under the quarter round). Record measurements in feet and inches and convert to decimal feet by dividing the inches by 12 — 11 ft 4 in is 11.33 ft, not 11.4.

Measure each dimension in two places, near each end of the room. If they differ, use the larger figure. If you are unfamiliar with breaking up odd shapes, our guide on [how to calculate square footage](/blog/how-to-calculate-square-footage) explains L-shapes, angled walls and bay windows.

## Step 3: Add closets, alcoves and doorways

These small areas add up:

- **Closets:** Measure them like separate small rooms. A 6 × 2 ft reach-in closet is 12 sq ft — nearly a full box of some products.
- **Doorways:** The new floor usually ends at the centre of the door slab when it is closed, where a transition strip sits. Add the doorway as a small rectangle (width × half the wall thickness, or the full depth if the floor continues).
- **Alcoves and bay windows:** Treat as extra rectangles or trapezoids.
- **Hallways:** Measure once, and decide which room "owns" any shared doorway so you don't count it twice.

## Step 4: Decide what to leave out

Not every bit of floor gets the new material:

- **Fixed kitchen cabinets and islands:** Floating floors (most laminate and click vinyl) need to move with temperature and humidity, so manufacturers typically say not to install them under fixed cabinets. Measure to the toe-kick.
- **Refrigerator and range spaces:** Include these. The floor continues under freestanding appliances.
- **Bathtubs and shower bases:** Leave out.
- **Stairs:** Measure separately. Stair nosings and treads are sold as individual pieces for most hard flooring.

## Step 5: Check whether the room is square

Measure both diagonals of the room (corner to opposite corner). If they are equal, the room is square. If they differ by more than about half an inch, the walls are out of square and your first and last rows will need tapered cuts, which increases waste. In that case, lean towards the higher end of the waste range below.

While you are at it, check the subfloor for flatness with a long straightedge or level. Floating floors are sensitive to dips and humps; many manufacturers specify a maximum variation of around 3/16 in over 10 ft, but check the installation instructions for your product. Low spots may need a patch or [floor leveling compound](/calculators/floor-leveling-compound-calculator).

## Step 6: Add a waste allowance

Every installation produces offcuts: the end of each row, cuts around door frames, and boards with damaged tongues. How much extra to buy depends on the layout:

| Layout and room | Typical waste allowance |
|---|---|
| Straight planks, simple rectangular room | 5–10% |
| Straight planks, many corners, closets or angled walls | 10–15% |
| Diagonal layout | 15% |
| Herringbone or chevron | 15–20% or more |
| Wide or long planks with large pattern variation | Add a few percent for board selection |

The [material waste factor guide](/blog/material-waste-factor-guide) explains where these numbers come from and how they compare across materials.

## Step 7: Convert to boxes

Flooring is sold by the box, and box coverage varies widely between products — anywhere from under 15 to over 30 sq ft. Divide your total (including waste) by the box coverage and **always round up** to a whole box.

## Worked example: a bedroom with a closet

From the plan above:

- **Room:** 12 ft 9 in × 11 ft 4 in = 12.75 × 11.33 = **144.5 sq ft**
- **Closet:** 6 ft × 2 ft = **12 sq ft**
- **Doorway:** 32 in wide × 2.25 in to centre of door ≈ 2.67 × 0.19 = **0.5 sq ft** (small, but include it)
- **Net area:** 144.5 + 12 + 0.5 = **157 sq ft**
- **Waste:** the room is square and the layout is straight, but there is a closet, so use 10%: 157 × 1.10 = **172.7 sq ft**
- **Boxes:** the chosen laminate covers 20.3 sq ft per box: 172.7 ÷ 20.3 = 8.5 → **9 boxes** (182.7 sq ft)

The extra 10 sq ft beyond the waste allowance is your spare stock. Keep at least a few planks — ideally an unopened box — for future repairs.

### Underlayment

Underlayment is sold in rolls by square feet. Use the net area plus a small overlap allowance (rolls are usually overlapped or taped at the seams per the product instructions). Some vinyl and laminate products have attached underlayment, in which case you may only need a vapour barrier over concrete. The [underlayment calculator](/calculators/underlayment-calculator) handles roll sizes.

### Baseboard and quarter round

Trim is measured in linear feet, not square feet. Measure the perimeter of the room and closet, then subtract door openings:

- Room perimeter: 2 × (12.75 + 11.33) = 48.2 ft
- Subtract the 32-in doorway (2.7 ft) and the 6-ft closet opening: 48.2 − 8.7 = 39.5 ft
- Add the three closet walls (6 + 2 + 2 = 10 ft): **49.5 linear ft**
- Add 10% for cuts and mitres: **about 54.5 linear ft**, or seven 8-ft lengths

The [baseboard and trim calculator](/calculators/baseboard-trim-calculator) does this for lengths of 8, 12 or 16 ft.

## Measuring several rooms

For a whole floor, keep a table:

| Room | Net sq ft | Waste % | Total sq ft |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bedroom 1 | 157 | 10% | 172.7 |
| Bedroom 2 | 132 | 10% | 145.2 |
| Hallway | 42 | 15% | 48.3 |
| **Total** | **331** | | **366.2** |

Add the totals for the same product together *before* converting to boxes — rounding up room by room over-buys. At 20.3 sq ft per box, 366.2 sq ft is 18.04 boxes, so 19 boxes. The [room-by-room flooring estimator](/calculators/room-by-room-flooring-estimator) is built for exactly this.

Hallways get a higher allowance because long, narrow spaces with several doorways create more short offcuts.

## Choosing the material

If you have not picked a product yet, your measurements may influence the choice — wet areas, basements and pet-heavy households suit some floors better than others. Our comparison of [laminate vs vinyl plank flooring](/blog/laminate-vs-vinyl-plank-flooring) covers water resistance, durability and cost.

## Before you order

- Confirm the box coverage of the exact product and colour.
- Order everything from the same production lot where possible, and check lot numbers on delivery.
- Let the flooring acclimate in the room if the manufacturer requires it.
- Double-check the transition pieces you need at each doorway and where floor heights change.

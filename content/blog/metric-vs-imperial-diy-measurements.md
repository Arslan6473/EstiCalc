Most home-improvement projects in the US still run on feet and inches, but metric shows up everywhere: European tile, imported flooring, cabinet hardware, appliance specs, online tutorials from other countries, and the millimeter scale printed on the back of your tape measure. Switching between the two systems isn't hard, but a few specific mistakes cause most of the trouble, and they can turn into a wrong-size order.

This guide gives the exact conversion factors that matter for DIY work, explains why area and volume conversions catch people out, and covers the "nominal vs actual" sizes that make conversions even trickier. The [Unit Converter](/calculators/unit-converter) handles quick conversions.

## The exact length conversions

Since 1959, the inch has been defined in terms of the meter:

- **1 inch = 25.4 mm exactly**
- **1 foot = 304.8 mm = 0.3048 m exactly**
- **1 yard = 0.9144 m exactly**
- **1 meter ≈ 3.2808 feet ≈ 39.37 inches**
- **1 mm ≈ 0.03937 inch**

Because these are exact, you can convert lengths with no rounding error until you choose to round.

### Useful fractions in millimeters

| Inch | Millimeters |
|---|---|
| 1/16 in | 1.6 mm |
| 1/8 in | 3.2 mm |
| 1/4 in | 6.4 mm |
| 3/8 in | 9.5 mm |
| 1/2 in | 12.7 mm |
| 3/4 in | 19.1 mm |
| 1 in | 25.4 mm |

Tile spacers, drill bits and plywood thicknesses are often described in both, so this table is handy to keep near the workbench.

## Area: square the factor

The most common conversion mistake in DIY is using a length factor on an area.

![A 1 meter square divided to show it equals 3.28 feet by 3.28 feet, or 10.764 square feet, illustrating why area conversion factors are squared](/images/blog/metric-vs-imperial-diy-measurements/diagram-1.svg)

A square that is 1 meter on each side is 3.2808 feet on each side, so its area is 3.2808 × 3.2808 = **10.764 square feet**, not 3.28.

| Convert | Multiply by |
|---|---|
| sq ft → m² | 0.0929 |
| m² → sq ft | 10.764 |
| sq in → cm² | 6.4516 |
| sq yd → m² | 0.8361 |
| m² → sq yd | 1.196 |
| acre → m² | 4,046.9 |

For converting within the imperial system, remember that 1 square yard is 9 square feet (3 × 3), not 3. The [Square Feet to Square Yards Calculator](/calculators/square-feet-to-square-yards-calculator) covers that, and our guide on [how to calculate square footage](/blog/how-to-calculate-square-footage) covers measuring.

## Volume: cube the factor

Volume adds a third dimension, so the factor is cubed:

| Convert | Multiply by |
|---|---|
| cu ft → m³ | 0.02832 |
| m³ → cu ft | 35.315 |
| cu yd → m³ | 0.7646 |
| m³ → cu yd | 1.308 |
| US gallon → liters | 3.785 |
| liter → US gallons | 0.2642 |
| cu ft → liters | 28.317 |

A cubic yard is 27 cubic feet (3 × 3 × 3). A cubic meter is a little more than a cubic yard, about 1.31 cubic yards. That matters when you read an overseas tutorial that says "order 2 cubic meters of soil": that's about 2.6 cubic yards. See [cubic yards explained](/blog/cubic-yards-explained) for more on bulk volumes.

### US vs imperial gallons

US and UK (imperial) gallons are different: a US gallon is about 3.785 liters, and an imperial gallon is about 4.546 liters. If you follow a British paint or pool-chemical guide, check which gallon it means. For paint coverage, [how much paint do I need](/blog/how-much-paint-do-i-need) uses US gallons.

## Weight

- **1 pound = 0.4536 kg**
- **1 kg = 2.2046 lb**
- **1 US short ton = 2,000 lb ≈ 907 kg**
- **1 metric tonne = 1,000 kg ≈ 2,205 lb**

A US "ton" of gravel and a metric "tonne" differ by about 10%, which matters when comparing prices or truck capacity.

## Nominal vs actual sizes

Converting a label doesn't always give the real dimension, because many building products are named by a nominal size.

### Lumber

North American dimensional lumber is named by its rough-sawn size before drying and planing:

| Nominal | Actual (inches) | Actual (mm, approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 × 4 | 1.5 × 3.5 | 38 × 89 |
| 2 × 6 | 1.5 × 5.5 | 38 × 140 |
| 2 × 8 | 1.5 × 7.25 | 38 × 184 |
| 2 × 10 | 1.5 × 9.25 | 38 × 235 |
| 4 × 4 | 3.5 × 3.5 | 89 × 89 |

Converting "2 × 4" as 2 inches × 4 inches would give 51 × 102 mm, which is wrong.

### Sheet goods

US plywood and drywall sheets are 4 × 8 feet (1,219 × 2,438 mm). In many metric countries, the common sheet is 1,200 × 2,400 mm, about 3/4 inch narrower and 1.5 inches shorter. Framing in those countries is often laid out at 600 or 400 mm centers instead of 24 or 16 inches. Mixing a metric sheet with imperial framing means edges won't land on studs. Our [drywall estimating guide](/blog/drywall-estimating-guide) assumes US sheet sizes.

Plywood thickness is also nominal: "3/4-inch" plywood is often slightly thinner, such as 23/32 inch.

### Tile

Tile sold as "12 × 12" may be metric 300 × 300 mm (11.81 in) or a nominal size slightly different from 12 inches. Over a 12-foot run of tiles, a 3/16-inch difference per tile adds up to about 2.25 inches. Always plan layout with the actual measured size. [How to estimate tile and grout](/blog/how-to-estimate-tile-and-grout) covers layout math.

## Worked example: converting a metric flooring plan

You've found a flooring plan for a room listed as **4.2 m × 3.6 m**, and the flooring you want is sold in boxes of 22.5 sq ft.

1. Area in metric: 4.2 × 3.6 = **15.12 m²**
2. Convert: 15.12 × 10.764 = **162.8 sq ft**
3. Add 10% waste: 162.8 × 1.10 = **179.0 sq ft**
4. Boxes: 179.0 ÷ 22.5 = 7.96 → **8 boxes**

**The mistake to avoid:** converting each side first and rounding hard. 4.2 m = 13.78 ft and 3.6 m = 11.81 ft; if you round those down to 13 ft and 11 ft you get 143 sq ft, nearly 20 sq ft short. If you convert sides, keep at least two decimal places or round up. For more on measuring a room, see [how to measure a room for flooring](/blog/how-to-measure-a-room-for-flooring), and for waste percentages see the [material waste factor guide](/blog/material-waste-factor-guide).

![Side-by-side comparison of a 4.2 by 3.6 meter room converted correctly to 162.8 square feet versus rounding the sides down to 13 by 11 feet and getting only 143 square feet](/images/blog/metric-vs-imperial-diy-measurements/diagram-2.svg)

## Tips for working in both systems

- **Pick one system per project** and convert everything into it before calculating.
- **Convert at the end.** Do the area or volume math in the original units, then convert the result once.
- **Keep precision until the final step.** Round only the final quantity, and round up for materials.
- **Use a dual-scale tape measure** to read metric dimensions directly.
- **Write units on every number.** "12" is meaningless without "in," "ft" or "mm."
- **Check actual sizes** of tile, sheet goods and lumber rather than converting the label.
- **Watch the decimal separator.** Many countries write 4,2 m for 4.2 m.

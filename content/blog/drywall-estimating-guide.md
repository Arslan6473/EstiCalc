Drywall estimating looks simple — divide square feet by 32 — but a good estimate also accounts for which sheet length suits each wall, how the seams fall, and the "small stuff" that makes up much of the finishing work: joint compound, tape, screws and corner bead. Running out of mud on a Sunday evening is no fun, and neither is a garage full of extra sheets you can't return.

This guide covers sheet types, two ways to count sheets, and rules of thumb for each consumable, finishing with a complete worked example. The [drywall sheets calculator](/calculators/drywall-sheets-calculator) does the sheet maths for you.

## Choose the right sheet

### Sizes

| Sheet | Area | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| 4 × 8 ft | 32 sq ft | Easy to carry; small rooms, DIY, vertical hanging on 8-ft walls |
| 4 × 10 ft | 40 sq ft | 10-ft walls hung vertically; walls between 8 and 10 ft long |
| 4 × 12 ft | 48 sq ft | Fewer seams on long walls and ceilings; heavy, needs two people |
| 54 in × 12 ft | 54 sq ft | 9-ft walls hung horizontally in two rows |

Longer sheets mean fewer butt joints, which are the hardest seams to finish flat. If you can get 12-ft sheets into the room — check stairwells and doorways — they usually save finishing time.

### Thickness and type

- **1/2 in:** Standard for interior walls in most homes.
- **5/8 in:** Stiffer and better at resisting sag on ceilings, particularly with framing 24 in on centre, and better at sound reduction. **Type X** fire-rated board is usually 5/8 in.
- **1/4 in and 3/8 in:** Curved walls and covering over existing surfaces.
- **Moisture- and mould-resistant board:** For bathrooms and laundry rooms outside the direct-wet zone.
- **Cement board or a waterproofing system:** For tile in showers and tub surrounds — standard drywall is not a tile backer for wet areas.

Building codes commonly require fire-rated board in specific places, such as between an attached garage and the house. Check your local code before you buy.

## Measure walls and ceilings

1. **Walls:** perimeter × wall height. A 12 × 14 ft room with 8-ft walls has 52 × 8 = 416 sq ft of wall.
2. **Ceiling:** length × width (12 × 14 = 168 sq ft).
3. **Openings:** Subtract only large openings (patio doors, wide archways). For ordinary doors and windows, the pieces you cut out are rarely reusable, so most estimators leave them in as built-in waste.

For walls with gables or slopes, see our guide on [how to calculate square footage](/blog/how-to-calculate-square-footage) for triangles and trapezoids.

## Two ways to count sheets

### The area method (quick)

> **Sheets = total area × 1.10 ÷ sheet area**

The 10% covers offcuts and mistakes. Use 15% for rooms with many corners, soffits or angled ceilings. It is quick and good for budgets, but can over- or under-count on specific walls. The [material waste factor guide](/blog/material-waste-factor-guide) compares drywall's allowance with other materials.

### The layout method (accurate)

Count sheets wall by wall, thinking about how they will actually be hung:

- Walls up to 8 ft 1 in tall take two horizontal rows of 4-ft sheets.
- For each row, pick the sheet length that spans the wall with the fewest joints.
- Offcuts longer than about 2 ft can often fill short sections elsewhere.

![A 14 foot wall, 8 feet tall, covered with two horizontal rows: the top row is a 12 foot sheet plus a 2 foot piece, the bottom row a 2 foot 8 inch piece plus a 12 foot sheet trimmed to 11 feet 4 inches, so the butt joints are staggered and fall on studs](/images/blog/drywall-estimating-guide/wall-layout.svg)

Every butt joint must land on a stud (or on added backing), so trim pieces to suit the stud layout. Stagger the butt joints between the upper and lower rows so they don't line up, and avoid placing a joint directly at the edge of a door or window opening, where cracks tend to form.

## Joint compound (mud)

Compound quantity depends on the number of seams, the finish level and your technique. A widely used estimating figure is:

> **About 140 lb of premixed all-purpose compound per 1,000 sq ft of drywall** for tape plus two finish coats.

That works out to roughly 4.5 lb per 4 × 8 sheet. Premixed compound is sold in buckets and boxes of around 4.5 gallons; the weight varies by product (lightweight compound weighs noticeably less), so convert using the weight printed on the container. The [joint compound calculator](/calculators/joint-compound-calculator) handles this conversion.

Many finishers use setting-type (powder) compound for the tape coat and corners because it hardens chemically and shrinks less, then switch to lightweight premixed for the finish coats. Buy for the method you will use.

### Finish level matters

The drywall industry describes finish levels from 0 (no taping) to 5 (full skim coat). Level 4 is typical for walls that will get flat or eggshell paint. Level 5 — a thin skim coat over the whole surface — is recommended under glossy paint or harsh side lighting, and can roughly double the compound you need. Our [paint sheen guide](/blog/paint-sheen-guide) explains why glossy finishes show every flaw.

## Tape

A common rule of thumb is **about 370–400 ft of joint tape per 1,000 sq ft of drywall**. Paper tape comes in rolls of around 250 and 500 ft. Mesh tape is popular with setting compound and for patching; paper tape is generally preferred for inside corners.

## Screws

Fastener spacing is set by building code and the manufacturer's instructions — for single-layer drywall on wood framing it is commonly up to 12 in apart on ceilings and 16 in on walls. In practice that works out to roughly **1 screw per square foot**, or about 32 per 4 × 8 sheet.

| Board thickness | Typical screw (wood framing) |
|---|---|
| 1/2 in | 1-1/4 in coarse-thread |
| 5/8 in | 1-5/8 in coarse-thread |
| Any, on light-gauge steel studs | Fine-thread, self-tapping per stud gauge |

Screws are sold by weight, and the count per pound varies by length and gauge; check the approximate count on the box. The [drywall screws calculator](/calculators/drywall-screws-calculator) estimates boxes.

## Corner bead and other extras

- **Corner bead:** One piece per outside corner, sold in 8- and 10-ft lengths. Count every outside corner, including window returns if they are drywalled.
- **Inside corner tape or flexible bead** for angled ceilings.
- **Sanding sponges or screens, a dust mask and drop cloths.**
- **Primer** formulated for new drywall before painting — see [how much paint do I need](/blog/how-much-paint-do-i-need).

## Worked example: a 12 × 14 ft bedroom

Walls are 8 ft high, with one door and two windows. We will use 4 × 12 ft sheets.

**Area:** walls 416 sq ft + ceiling 168 sq ft = **584 sq ft**.

**Area method:** 584 × 1.10 = 642 sq ft ÷ 48 = 13.4 → **14 sheets** of 4 × 12.

**Layout method:**

- Two 12-ft walls × 2 rows = **4 sheets** (one sheet per row, no joints)
- Two 14-ft walls × 2 rows = **4 sheets** (two trimmed to 11 ft 4 in so the joints land on studs) plus fill pieces of 2 ft and 2 ft 8 in for each wall — 9 ft 4 in of fill in total, which **1 more sheet** provides
- Ceiling: 12-ft sheets running across the 12-ft width, perpendicular to joists spanning the 14-ft direction: 14 ÷ 4 = 3.5 rows → **4 sheets**

Total: **13 sheets of 4 × 12**, or 624 sq ft. Add one spare for a damaged sheet or a bad cut and you have 14 — the same as the area method, but now you know how the job goes together and which offcuts get used where.

**Consumables** (based on 624 sq ft of board):

| Item | Calculation | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Premixed compound | 624 × 0.14 = 87 lb | 2 boxes or buckets |
| Paper tape | 624 × 0.4 = 250 ft | one 500-ft roll |
| Screws (1-1/4 in) | about 1 per sq ft ≈ 625 | box with 650+ screws |
| Corner bead | Only if the windows have drywall returns: 4 edges per window | 8 pieces for 2 windows |

Material prices vary by region and change often, so price the list at your local supplier. The [drywall cost calculator](/calculators/drywall-cost-calculator) helps compare doing it yourself with hiring a crew.

## Safety and practical notes

- Drywall is heavy: a 4 × 12 sheet of 1/2 in board weighs roughly 70–80 lb, and 5/8 in more. Use a drywall lift for ceilings and get a helper.
- Before screwing into walls, make sure wiring and pipes are protected by steel nail plates where they pass through studs close to the face. Turn off power to boxes you are working around.
- Moving, adding or removing walls and any electrical work may need a permit and inspection. Hire a licensed electrician for wiring changes.
- Sanding compound creates fine dust. Wear a properly fitted respirator rated for fine dust and seal off the work area.
